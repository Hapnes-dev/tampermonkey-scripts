# Rocketlane Day Recap

Adds a 🏭 **Plants visited** button on Rocketlane's **My Timesheet** — pick a date and see every IWMAC plant you visited that day (plant_id, plant name, first/last action time, which actions you performed — shown as friendly labels with a colour-coded category dot (e.g. `direct_plant` → "Direct", `pma_local` → "phpMyAdmin", `upload` → "Backup") — and an estimated time split). At the top, a 📋 **Day by category** roll-up estimates how your plant time splits across your Rocketlane task categories (Integration, Drawing & Design, Setup, Support), ready to copy straight into your timesheet.

## Install

[Click here to install](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hapnes-dev/tampermonkey-scripts/main/rocketlane-day-recap/Rocketlane-Day-Recap.user.js) (requires Tampermonkey).

## Usage

1. On any `https://kiona.rocketlane.com/timesheets/...` page, click 🏭 **Plants visited** (bottom-right)
2. Pick a date
3. Your visits for that date appear automatically — instantly from cache if you've scanned it before, otherwise a quick scan of your recent pang plants **plus every plant you've been found on before**. This footprint grows with each scan, so over time it covers your real working set — including plant-admin/designer plants — without a full scan. Click **Refresh** to re-scan the selected date (fast) and update its cache — handy for *today*, as more activity gets logged through the day.
4. For a complete picture — to discover brand-new plants not yet in your footprint — click **🔍 Full scan**. It queries all ~7,600 plants (~1 min, after a one-time confirmation). Because pang returns each plant's *entire* history, one full scan **caches every date you worked** — so browsing the rest of that month (or any past date in it) is then instant — and adds every plant found to your footprint.

### 📋 Day by category

Above the plant list, a **Day by category** panel rolls your visits up into the same task categories you book in Rocketlane (`Project - Integration`, `Project - Drawing & Design`, `Setup - PC / Gateway`, `Support - External`), with an estimated hours bar per category and a **⧉ Copy** button that yields paste-ready `Category: H h` lines. Each individual plant row also shows its **own** category split as small colour-dot chips (e.g. `Integration 53m · Drawing 24m · Setup 18m`), so you can see which plant fed each category. The mapping reads the *kind* of work, not just the clicks: **Designer** and **AK3-scanner setup** log very few pang clicks but take real time, so each is credited a nominal chunk (Designer ≈ 8 min/use, AK3 ≈ 18 min/use) carved off the visit's minutes; the remainder goes to **Integration** when there's real config evidence (a non-graphic config commit, phpMyAdmin, or System tools), to **Drawing** if the visit was graphic-only, to **Setup** if it was AK3-only, otherwise a short access-only visit counts as a quick **Support** check. Crucially, adding a device commits the graphic table *and* the device tables together, so a commit only counts as Drawing evidence when it touched the graphic table and nothing else — Drawing time therefore comes from the Designer *actions*. This is **plant work only**: meetings, admin/planning, documentation and training never touch pang, so they're left out — add those lines yourself. Treat the hours as an estimate and adjust before submitting.

The time split is estimated from your clicks (pang logs clicks, not active time), with one refinement from the change log: a **sub-tool config session** — phpMyAdmin / Designer / Direct / VNC work that logs almost no pang clicks but commits a real config change — would otherwise show as ~1 min. When a sparse-click visit that opened one of those tools has a real config commit, it's credited the actual session span (first click → the commit, 5–20 min) instead. Click-heavy plants are unchanged. Conversely, a **single** phpMyAdmin or System-tools click that committed *nothing* is treated as a quick check (capped at ~8 min) rather than inheriting the full 30-min gap credit — a refinement validated against 30 days of real activity. Optionally set your **Workday total** and tick **Distribute to total** to split the day's hours across the plants weighted by that activity.

Each plant row also shows a 🔧 **changes** badge when IWMAC recorded config commits *during your active window* on that plant — a fast way to tell plants you actually configured from ones you only opened. **Click the badge** to expand a drawer showing *what* changed, read live from pang's config-commit history. The drawer groups changes into collapsible sections, **all collapsed by default** so you can drill into just what you care about — in order: **Plant units** (named by the unit, e.g. `Belimo Energimåler (1)`), **Graphic** (e.g. `Graphic Hovedoversikt: rev 4 → 5 · layout edited`), **Plant settings** (e.g. `packet_interval (AK3): 400 → 4000`), and **More changes** (driver parameters, devices, virtual values…). Click a section header to expand it; long sections reveal ~10 changes at a time behind a clickable **“+N more changes”** link, so you can keep clicking to dig deeper without being flooded. Lines are colour-coded: green = added, red = removed, blue = changed. When a lot of units are added or removed at once the line collapses to a count (`+ 38 units added`) with a **“show all”** link to list every one, and a bulk identical change in *More changes* (e.g. a regroup that bumps the alarm group on dozens of rows) collapses to a single `group: 3 → 6 (44 rows)` line instead of dozens of near-identical ones. The drawer also **keeps its place**: if you expand sections or reveal more lines and then change the workday total (which redraws the list), it reopens exactly where you left off. Commits are automatic `:system:` snapshots with no author recorded, so this shows what the config changed to *around* your visit — not necessarily changes you personally made. The badge counts only **change-triggered** commits: scheduled snapshots (the hourly `:00`/`:01` ones, plus nightly and daily) are filtered out as noise, so a long visit no longer over-counts just because an hourly snapshot happened to land inside it.

## How it works

The panel runs on Rocketlane and calls pang's `actions.php` (`method:"get_history"`) once per plant in scope via `GM_xmlhttpRequest` (batched, http origin — see below). **Search** scopes to your recent plants plus your accumulated footprint (`user_plants`: every plant you've been matched on); **Full scan** scopes to the whole inventory and feeds that footprint. Since pang has no server-side date filter, there's no way to shrink the per-plant response — keeping the everyday scope small (footprint) is what keeps it fast. For the date on screen it additionally calls the changes backend (`commits.php`, `method:"get_commits"`) for just the shown plants and tags each with any config commits that land inside your active window (the 🔧 badge); those commits are automatic `:system:` snapshots, so the badge is a time-correlation, not an authored-by signal. Each commit is classified by its timestamp — scheduled snapshots (hourly `:00`/`:01`, nightly, daily) vs off-the-hour change-triggered ones — and only the change-triggered ones count toward the badge; a change-triggered commit on a sparse sub-tool session also feeds the time estimate (see above). Clicking the badge drills in via two more changes services — `tables.php` (`get_tables_patch`: which tables a commit touched, with a `mode` flag) and `data.php` (`get_two_versions`: old vs new content) — classifying noise/blobs/added-devices by table name *before* fetching, so only real parameter tables are downloaded and diffed (by column name, so structural changes don't misalign).

The full inventory (plant ids + names) only exists in a live pang tab, websocket-loaded into `module_plants.coll.data` — there's no HTTP endpoint that lists plants. So the first full scan briefly opens `pang.qxs` in the foreground to harvest that list, then caches it for reuse.

## Limitations

- Pang's API is per-plant (`get_history(plant_id)`) and has no server-side "what did user X do on date Y" filter, so every plant's full history is read and filtered client-side. To keep this fast the script **batches** the reads (JSON-RPC, up to ~30 plants per request, 20 concurrent) — pang processes a batch sequentially, so it's the same server load as single calls but with far fewer round-trips (~per-plant time roughly halves; a Full scan is a few hundred requests, not ~7,600). Full-scan results are cached per date (`full_scan_cache`), so re-opening a previously full-scanned day is instant. Today's cache can go stale as you keep working — the footer shows the scan time, and Full scan always re-runs and overwrites it.
- The plant list itself only exists in a live pang tab (websocket-loaded into `module_plants.coll.data`; no HTTP endpoint, and IDs are sparse — 203 up past 50000 — so a numeric range scan isn't viable). The first full scan opens pang in the foreground for ~6 s to harvest the inventory, then caches it (`all_plants`), so the flash rarely repeats.
- **Refresh** (and the default auto-load) is fast but only covers your ~50 recent pang plants plus your footprint — it misses any plant you didn't open through pang (e.g. plant-admin/designer visits). Use **Full scan** to catch those.
- pang is served on both **http and https**, with *separate* per-origin browser storage — so the recent list and login differ between the two (the full inventory + history are identical). The script copies recent + login into shared Tampermonkey storage and, on first use, harvests from **both** protocols, so it works whichever one a colleague uses.
- **Identity matching.** History entries are tagged with a `user` field that pang derives from the auth cookie (`iw_security[username]`) — bare for some logins (`thomas.kvalvag`), email for others (`eivind.slordal@kiona.com`). The script reads that same cookie (not the SPA's `pang.login.username`, which can be missing or formatted differently) and normalises both sides (lowercase, strip `@domain`) before matching. If a date has no activity for you, the panel simply shows a **"No data for &lt;date&gt;"** message (a Quick scan also nudges you to try a Full scan, since your visit may be on a plant not in your recent list).
