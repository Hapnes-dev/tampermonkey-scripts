# Rocketlane Day Recap

Adds a 🏭 **Plants visited** button on Rocketlane's **My Timesheet** — pick a date and see every IWMAC plant you visited that day (plant_id, plant name, first/last action time, which actions you performed — shown as friendly labels with a colour-coded category dot (e.g. `direct_plant` → "Direct", `pma_local` → "phpMyAdmin", `upload` → "Backup") — and an estimated time split). At the top, a 📋 **Day by category** roll-up estimates how your plant time splits across your Rocketlane task categories (Integration, Drawing & Design, Setup), ready to copy straight into your timesheet. Short "just popped in to check" visits show as a separate **Quick check** line — left out of the booked total, since it's your call whether they're worth logging.

## Install

[Click here to install](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hapnes-dev/tampermonkey-scripts/main/rocketlane-day-recap/Rocketlane-Day-Recap.user.js) (requires Tampermonkey).

## Usage

1. On any `https://kiona.rocketlane.com/timesheets/...` page, click 🏭 **Plants visited** (bottom-right). Drag the panel's dark header to move it anywhere — it remembers the spot; double-click the header to snap it back to the corner.
2. Pick a date
3. Your visits for that date appear automatically. They come from **IWMAC All logs** — one query that returns everything you did on that date across the whole fleet, including work pang's click log never records: handover **notes**, **operations-log** entries (alarm settings, duty lists) and **service logons**. Those show up as extra action chips, as a 📝 line on the plant row, and in the notes of anything you book. (A note that looks like it contains a password is shown as `[redacted]` — the link in it is kept, the value never travels.) All logs needs you to be logged into `internal.iwmac.local`; when it isn't reachable the panel falls back to the cached full scan, then to a quick scan of your recent pang plants **plus every plant you've been found on before**. That footprint grows with each scan, so it covers your real working set — including plant-admin/designer plants — without a full scan. Click **Refresh** to re-read the selected date — handy for *today*, as more activity gets logged through the day.
4. **Once a day, the panel recommends a full scan.** The first time you open it on a given day, an amber tip sits above the list: *"No full scan yet today"* — with a **🔍 Run full scan** button and a **Not today** dismiss. It's there because the quick data can't see plant-admin or designer visits, and one scan caches every date it finds, so a single sweep a day keeps the whole week honest. As soon as a full scan has run — from that button, from **🔍 Full scan**, or as part of **⤴ Book week** — the tip disappears for the rest of the day, and it comes back the next day.
5. For a complete picture — to discover brand-new plants not yet in your footprint — click **🔍 Full scan**. It queries all ~7,600 plants (~1 min, after a one-time confirmation), scanning your own plants first and showing a live `X of Y plants · Z found` counter as it goes. If any plants are unreachable mid-scan (after an automatic retry), the result is shown but **not cached**, with a ⚠ warning in the footer — so a network hiccup can't silently poison the cached month. Because pang returns each plant's *entire* history, one full scan **caches every date you worked** — so browsing the rest of that month (or any past date in it) is then instant — and adds every plant found to your footprint.

### 📋 Day by category

Above the plant list, a **Day by category** panel rolls your visits up into the same task categories you book in Rocketlane (`Project - Integration`, `Project - Drawing & Design`, `Setup - PC / Gateway`), with an estimated hours bar per category, a **⧉ Copy** button that yields paste-ready `Category: H h` lines, and a **⤴ Book day** button that **writes the entries straight into your timesheet**: one entry per plant & category, the project matched by plant-id prefix (`2184 - …`), booked onto the **best-fitting existing project task** when one exists (`Design: Wireless overview` for the drawing you changed, `Integration: Refrigeration` / a bare discipline task like `Wireless` for integration — the what-changed text goes in the entry notes) and only **creating a new activity when nothing fits**, with the text describing what actually changed — added devices for Integration ("Integration: added Grundfos CIM200"), the **changed drawing's name** for Drawing ("Drawing: Wireless Overview") — and the required Rocketlane category set. You review the plan first — every bookable row has a **tickbox** (ticked by default), so untick anything you don't want synced (per-row: tick / already booked ⏭) — then one click books the ticked ones via Rocketlane's own API. A plant with **no project of its own** gets a picker over your **Team … Oppgaver** bucket projects instead: choose one (remembered as next time's default) and it books there — when the bucket has an **"Oppgaver utenfor Rocketlane"** task (like Team Kulde Oppgaver), the entry lands on the plant's **subtask** under it (`<plant id> - <plant name>`, found or created automatically *underneath* that container task, matching the list convention) — and each booking also appends a dated line of what you did to the subtask's **Description**, so it doubles as the plant's visit log — self-healing: if a booking predates this feature (or a write once failed), the missing dated lines are filled in automatically the next time you open that day's booking review, ⏭ already-booked rows included; otherwise as an activity named `<plant id> <plant name> - <activity>`. *(Subtasks created before v4.108 were left sitting at the top level of the bucket project instead of under the container — they still work and still collect their visit log, but you'll want to drag them under "Oppgaver utenfor Rocketlane" once; Rocketlane's API can't move them. From 4.108 the script attaches them correctly and tells you in the console if one ever doesn't.)* Every entry also gets a **Notes** field that reads like a summary of what you did there that day, not a field dump. It opens with one plain sentence **written for the project leader** (v4.114) — *"Connected 2 new refrigeration controllers to the monitoring platform, adjusted control and alarm settings on 3 devices and adjusted plant-level settings."*, *"Updated 1 overview screen for the plant's operator display ("Oversikt_Øst")."*, *"Configured the plant's communication gateway (RAC) so the plant reports to the monitoring platform."*, or *"Remote follow-up on the plant — checked status and logs."* — with the discipline (refrigeration / ventilation / energy / wireless…) read from what the day actually changed and from your own log note. That first line is deliberate: Rocketlane's time-entry drawer only shows one line of the note until you click into it, so the visible part is the answer a non-technical reader needs rather than a label. Underneath it, anything you wrote in the IWMAC **operations log** or a handover note that day appears as `Log: …` lines, then the technical evidence: the engineer's one-line summary (*"Added 2 units (AK-CC55-017x 6 and Belimo Energimåler), tuned parameters on Data Engine and changed 1 plant setting (packet_interval)."*) followed by the full detail as before — added units by name, renames as `old → new`, which params were tuned per device, plant-setting changes **with values** (`packet_interval (AK3): 400 → 4000` — anything that looks like a password/secret shows only "changed"), and per-drawing `rev X → Y · layout edited`. Internal machinery (Data Engine / sysinfo tables) never appears as "work". **When the evidence genuinely can't decide, it follows your own habit** (v4.113): the panel reads the last 8 weeks of your timesheet and learns which task each project + category usually gets booked onto. That only ever breaks a *tie* — it can never overrule what actually changed in the plant — but it replaces what used to be an alphabetical guess. Measured against your own 16-week history, the most-booked task for a project + category is the one you pick **76%** of the time. **The matcher also reads what you wrote that day** (v4.112): the handover notes and operations-log comments the panel already collects from IWMAC All logs now count as evidence for *which* task to book onto — *"Byttet føler i kjøledisk 3"* points at the refrigeration work package, *"Service på ventilasjonsanlegg"* at ventilation. They rank below the hard evidence of what actually changed in the plant database, so they can never overrule it, but above unit names — and on a day that left no config commit at all (a phone day, a pure Designer day) they are the only evidence there is, where the pick used to be little better than alphabetical. When two same-discipline tasks tie, the one whose **name** the day's own text actually mentions wins (v4.114 — "byttet føler i kjøl rack 2" lands on `Integration: Kjøl`, not whichever tied task history happens to prefer), ranked above the habit prior and below decided evidence. A **Support - External** entry now books onto a clearly named support/service task when the project carries one (`Support og oppfølging`); most projects don't, and those keep the activity fallback. The task matcher understands Norwegian↔English discipline names and your device vocabulary (Danfoss 080Z/084B/EKC/AK-CC ⇒ refrigeration, Exhausto/OJ ⇒ ventilation, CGE/EM2 ⇒ energy — calibrated on 37 real plant-days and the standard project template): completed ✓ tasks lose evidence ties to open ones, on equal evidence the more specific task wins (`Design: Ventilation` beats `Design: Heating/ VGV`), a bare-named task like `Nytt oversiktsbilde` inherits its discipline from the board category it sits in (*Refrigeration and freezing systems*), and sales-order quantity lines (`Per energy meter — 3 pcs`, `IWMAC Aftermarket: …`) are never booking targets. **Creating a new activity is a last resort**: the matcher also knows Norwegian operational task names (`Konfigurering`, `Igangkjøring`, `Oppstart`, `Integrasjon: …`; `Server configured` / `Port forwarding` count as Setup), and when the strict match fails a rescue pass books onto any open task whose name **or board category** fits the day's evidence — and rather than give up on a tie it takes a deterministic **best guess** (marked *(best guess)* in the plan row so you can eyeball it before booking). Checklist rows (approvals, `Alarm test`, sales/handover/documentation) are never picked, and no two categories land on the same task. On finished projects where every task is ✓-completed, the guess books onto the completed task rather than creating an activity, and a day with no config evidence at all guesses by your discipline order (refrigeration first) — so a pure Designer day lands on `Design: Refrigeration`, not a `Drawing: Designer session` activity. Only a project with nothing bookable left still gets a new activity. Quick checks are never booked, a project+category already on that date is skipped (so re-clicking can't double-book), archived duplicate projects are ignored, and a **RAC** plant/project books its integration remainder under **Setup - PC / Gateway** instead. Each individual plant row also shows its **own** category split as small colour-dot chips (e.g. `Integration 53m · Drawing 24m · Setup 18m`), so you can see which plant fed each category. The mapping reads the *kind* of work, not just the clicks: **Designer** and **AK3-scanner setup** log very few pang clicks but take real time, so each is credited a nominal chunk (AK3 ≈ 18 min/use; **Designer = the designer session** — from opening the designer until you leave the plant, bridging a quick pop-out to phpMyAdmin/VNC and back but stopping at a real pause; and if the plant's own config **commit** lands within 20 min of the session's end, the session extends to that save (a quick glance at another plant no longer swallows the drawing time) — or 8 min, whichever is larger) carved off the visit's minutes; the remainder goes to **Integration** when there's real config evidence (a non-graphic config commit, phpMyAdmin, or System tools), to **Drawing** if the visit was graphic-only, to **Setup** if it was AK3-only, otherwise a short access-only visit (under ~15 min — you most likely just popped in to check something) is a **Quick check**: shown on the row and in the roll-up for awareness, but **left out of the booked total**. Crucially, adding a device commits the graphic table *and* the device tables together (measured: 457/457 config commits across 94 plants are device/integration changes), so a commit can never tell graphic work apart from device work — **Drawing time therefore comes from the Designer *actions* — specifically the time you spend in the designer between opening it and your next pang click**, not the commits. This is **plant work only**: meetings, admin/planning, documentation and training never touch pang, so they're left out — add those lines yourself. Treat the hours as an estimate and adjust before submitting.

**⤴ Book week** (v4.93): a button injected **left of Rocketlane's own "Add"** on the My Timesheet toolbar books the whole week in one go. It opens with a **full-scan check-up** (v4.116): if no full scan has run yet today, it says so and asks before building — **🔍 Full scan first** re-verifies every weekday in one sweep (days IWMAC All logs can answer skip the scan), **Use cached data** proceeds as before; once a scan has run today the question disappears and the finished build shows a green ✓ line naming its coverage ("All logs answered for every weekday" / "full scan completed" / "already ran today"). Beyond that it **guarantees full-scan data**: any weekday without a cached full scan triggers one automatic full scan covering all of them at once — a recent cache is trusted as-is (past days never re-scan; today only re-scans when its cache is older than 3 h) — so no plant-admin/designer visit can be missed — then it builds the booking plan for **Monday–Friday** (future days are skipped), distributes each day to your **Workday total (default 7,5 h)** across that day's bookable plants, and shows one review list — day headers with `N to book · 7h 30m · ⏭ M already booked`, a tickbox per row, `‹ ›` to switch weeks — then books everything ticked. **↻ re-reads the week** (v4.117): the header's refresh button rebuilds the shown week from scratch with a fresh sweep — it ignores the day's cached answers (All logs, config commits, and what's already on the sheet), so it's the way to pick up work you did *after* the last scan without closing the popup, and it stays available once a full scan has already run today and the check-up no longer asks. **It never duplicates**: anything already on the sheet for that project & category & date shows as ⏭ and is skipped — including plants already booked into a **team bucket** (recognised by their `<plant id> …` activity, shown ⏭ up front) — and if the what's-already-booked check fails for a day, that whole day is locked out rather than risk a double entry. A **cleared week is bookable**: an honestly-empty timesheet response is recognised as empty (clear a week, then refill it in one click). Plants without a project of their own get the same team-bucket picker as Book day, right in the week view: choose a **Team … Oppgaver** project (your last choice is preselected), tick the row, and it books there the same way as Book day (onto the plant's subtask under "Oppgaver utenfor Rocketlane" when the bucket has that task, else as the `<plant id> <plant name> - <activity>` activity). It's also robust against a flaky day: a transient server error while booking (5xx/429) first **verifies whether the entry actually landed** before retrying — so the retry can never double-book — and the status line narrates every phase (`Full scan — 4 100 of 7 663 plants…`, `looking up plant names…`, `reading what changed — plant 3 of 9…`), so a slow pang never looks like a hang.

The time split follows your **plant-to-plant transitions**: every click and log entry across all plants forms one timeline, and each plant is credited from your first action on it until you move to the next plant — with a 30-min cap per silent stretch so a lunch break isn't billed as plant work. The plant's own **change log** then sharpens the boundaries (v4.114): a config commit landing *inside* a silent stretch proves you were still on that plant, so the credit extends to that save (up to the 60-min trust horizon — commits carry no author, so trust decays with distance) instead of stopping at the flat 30; and when the plant that closed your day commits within 20 min of your final click, the day's end extends to that save too. Both only ever *add* time over the old estimate. Three older refinements still apply: a **sub-tool config session** — phpMyAdmin / Designer / Direct / VNC work that logs almost no pang clicks but commits a real config change — is credited the actual session span (first click → the commit, 5–20 min) instead of ~1 min, and if a long idle had already inflated the click estimate to the 30-min cap, the commit pulls it back **down** to that span; a **single** click that just opened a plant or a tool (Direct / VNC / phpMyAdmin / System tools) and committed *nothing* is treated as a quick check (capped at ~8 min) rather than inheriting the full 30-min gap credit; and a silence of over ~45 minutes with **no** commit anywhere in it counts as ~15 min — an unevidenced long silence is usually a break or a meeting. Optionally set your **Workday total** and tick **Distribute to total** to split the day's hours across your **bookable** plants weighted by that activity (quick checks are shown but kept out of the distributed total, so "to book" matches your workday).

Each plant row also shows a 🔧 **changes** badge when IWMAC recorded config commits *during your active window* on that plant — a fast way to tell plants you actually configured from ones you only opened. **Click the badge** to expand a drawer showing *what* changed, read live from pang's config-commit history. The drawer groups changes into collapsible sections, **all collapsed by default** so you can drill into just what you care about — in order: **Plant units** (named by the unit, e.g. `Belimo Energimåler (1)`), **Graphic** (e.g. `Graphic Hovedoversikt: rev 4 → 5 · layout edited`), **Plant settings** (e.g. `packet_interval (AK3): 400 → 4000`), and **More changes** (driver parameters, devices, virtual values…). Click a section header to expand it; long sections reveal ~10 changes at a time behind a clickable **“+N more changes”** link, so you can keep clicking to dig deeper without being flooded. Lines are colour-coded: green = added, red = removed, blue = changed. When a lot of units are added or removed at once the line collapses to a count (`+ 38 units added`) with a **“show all”** link to list every one, and a bulk identical change in *More changes* (e.g. a regroup that bumps the alarm group on dozens of rows) collapses to a single `group: 3 → 6 (44 rows)` line instead of dozens of near-identical ones. The drawer also **keeps its place**: if you expand sections or reveal more lines and then change the workday total (which redraws the list), it reopens exactly where you left off. Commits are automatic `:system:` snapshots with no author recorded, so this shows what the config changed to *around* your visit — not necessarily changes you personally made. The badge counts only **change-triggered** commits: scheduled snapshots (the hourly `:00`/`:01` ones, plus nightly and daily) are filtered out as noise, so a long visit no longer over-counts just because an hourly snapshot happened to land inside it.

## How it works

For the date on screen the panel first asks **IWMAC All logs** (`internal.iwmac.local/tools/all_logs/service.php`) for one day of your activity — a single request that covers every plant and includes the notes and operations-log entries pang has no record of. That call needs your `internal.iwmac.local` session, so it runs with cookies attached; if it can't be reached (off the office network, or you haven't logged in there) the panel falls back to the cached full scan and then to a pang scan, and if All logs reports that it truncated the reply, the two sources are merged rather than one being trusted. All logs never writes the full-scan cache — that cache still means "a pang sweep covered this date".

Everything else still comes from pang. The panel calls pang's `actions.php` (`method:"get_history"`) once per plant in scope via `GM_xmlhttpRequest` (batched, http origin — see below). **Search** scopes to your recent plants plus your accumulated footprint (`user_plants`: every plant you've been matched on); **Full scan** scopes to the whole inventory and feeds that footprint. Since pang has no server-side date filter, there's no way to shrink the per-plant response — keeping the everyday scope small (footprint) is what keeps it fast. For the date on screen it additionally calls the changes backend (`commits.php`, `method:"get_commits"`) for just the shown plants and tags each with any config commits that land inside your active window (the 🔧 badge); those commits are automatic `:system:` snapshots, so the badge is a time-correlation, not an authored-by signal. Each commit is classified by its timestamp — scheduled snapshots (hourly `:00`/`:01`, nightly, daily) vs off-the-hour change-triggered ones — and only the change-triggered ones count toward the badge; a change-triggered commit on a sparse sub-tool session also feeds the time estimate (see above). Clicking the badge drills in via two more changes services — `tables.php` (`get_tables_patch`: which tables a commit touched, with a `mode` flag) and `data.php` (`get_two_versions`: old vs new content) — classifying noise/blobs/added-devices by table name *before* fetching, so only real parameter tables are downloaded and diffed (by column name, so structural changes don't misalign).

The full inventory (plant ids + names) only exists in a live pang tab, websocket-loaded into `module_plants.coll.data` — there's no HTTP endpoint that lists plants. So the first full scan briefly opens `pang.qxs` in the foreground to harvest that list, then caches it for reuse.

## Limitations

- Pang's API is per-plant (`get_history(plant_id)`) and has no server-side "what did user X do on date Y" filter, so every plant's full history is read and filtered client-side. To keep this fast the script **batches** the reads (JSON-RPC, ~10 plants per request, 20 workers — measured 1.6× faster than bigger batches, since pang serializes each batch and large ones block the browser's ~6 connections; a Full scan lands around ~30 s). The changes log is fetched the same way but as **parallel single requests** (3.2× faster than a batch when the server's cache is cold) and each plant's commit list is **cached for the session** — one list covers every date, so browsing a week only fetches it once. Full-scan results are cached per date (`full_scan_cache`), so re-opening a previously full-scanned day is instant. Today's cache can go stale as you keep working — the footer shows the scan time, and Full scan always re-runs and overwrites it.
- The plant list itself only exists in a live pang tab (websocket-loaded into `module_plants.coll.data`; no HTTP endpoint, and IDs are sparse — 203 up past 50000 — so a numeric range scan isn't viable). The first full scan opens pang in the foreground for ~6 s to harvest the inventory, then caches it (`all_plants`), so the flash rarely repeats.
- **Refresh** (and the default auto-load) reads All logs, which covers every plant. When All logs is unavailable it falls back to your ~50 recent pang plants plus your footprint, which misses any plant you didn't open through pang (e.g. plant-admin/designer visits) — use **Full scan** to catch those. Full scan is also what keeps the multi-date cache and your plant footprint up to date, so the once-a-day recommendation still stands.
- pang is served on both **http and https**, with *separate* per-origin browser storage — so the recent list and login differ between the two (the full inventory + history are identical). The script copies recent + login into shared Tampermonkey storage and, on first use, harvests from **both** protocols, so it works whichever one a colleague uses.
- **Identity matching.** History entries are tagged with a `user` field that pang derives from the auth cookie (`iw_security[username]`) — bare for some logins (`thomas.kvalvag`), email for others (`eivind.slordal@kiona.com`). The script reads that same cookie (not the SPA's `pang.login.username`, which can be missing or formatted differently) and normalises both sides (lowercase, strip `@domain`) before matching. If a date has no activity for you, the panel simply shows a **"No data for &lt;date&gt;"** message (a Quick scan also nudges you to try a Full scan, since your visit may be on a plant not in your recent list).
