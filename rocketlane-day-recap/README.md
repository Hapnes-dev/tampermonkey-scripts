# Rocketlane Day Recap

Adds a 🏭 **Plants visited** button on Rocketlane's **My Timesheet** — pick a date and see every IWMAC plant you visited that day (plant_id, plant name, first/last action time, which actions you performed — shown as friendly labels with a colour-coded category dot (e.g. `direct_plant` → "Direct", `pma_local` → "phpMyAdmin", `upload` → "Backup") — and an estimated time split). At the top, a 📋 **Day by category** roll-up estimates how your plant time splits across your Rocketlane task categories (Integration, Drawing & Design, Setup), ready to copy straight into your timesheet. Short "just popped in to check" visits show as a separate **Quick check** line — left out of the booked total, since it's your call whether they're worth logging.

## Install

[Click here to install](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hapnes-dev/tampermonkey-scripts/main/rocketlane-day-recap/Rocketlane-Day-Recap.user.js) (requires Tampermonkey).

## Usage

1. On any `https://kiona.rocketlane.com/timesheets/...` page, click 🏭 **Plants visited** (bottom-right). Drag the panel's dark header to move it anywhere — it remembers the spot; double-click the header to snap it back to the corner.
2. Pick a date
3. Your visits for that date appear automatically — instantly from cache if you've scanned it before, otherwise a quick scan of your recent pang plants **plus every plant you've been found on before**. This footprint grows with each scan, so over time it covers your real working set — including plant-admin/designer plants — without a full scan. Click **Refresh** to re-scan the selected date (fast) and update its cache — handy for *today*, as more activity gets logged through the day.
4. For a complete picture — to discover brand-new plants not yet in your footprint — click **🔍 Full scan**. It queries all ~7,600 plants (~1 min, after a one-time confirmation), scanning your own plants first and showing a live `X of Y plants · Z found` counter as it goes. If any plants are unreachable mid-scan (after an automatic retry), the result is shown but **not cached**, with a ⚠ warning in the footer — so a network hiccup can't silently poison the cached month. Because pang returns each plant's *entire* history, one full scan **caches every date you worked** — so browsing the rest of that month (or any past date in it) is then instant — and adds every plant found to your footprint.

### 📋 Day by category

Above the plant list, a **Day by category** panel rolls your visits up into the same task categories you book in Rocketlane (`Project - Integration`, `Project - Drawing & Design`, `Setup - PC / Gateway`), with an estimated hours bar per category, a **⧉ Copy** button that yields paste-ready `Category: H h` lines, and a **⤴ Book day** button that **writes the entries straight into your timesheet**: one entry per plant & category, the project matched by plant-id prefix (`2184 - …`), booked onto the **best-fitting existing project task** when one exists (`Design: Wireless overview` for the drawing you changed, `Integration: Refrigeration` / a bare discipline task like `Wireless` for integration — the what-changed text goes in the entry notes) and only **creating a new activity when nothing fits**, with the text describing what actually changed — added devices for Integration ("Integration: added Grundfos CIM200"), the **changed drawing's name** for Drawing ("Drawing: Wireless Overview") — and the required Rocketlane category set. You review the plan first — every bookable row has a **tickbox** (ticked by default), so untick anything you don't want synced (per-row: tick / already booked ⏭) — then one click books the ticked ones via Rocketlane's own API. A plant with **no project of its own** gets a picker over your **Team … Oppgaver** bucket projects instead: choose one (remembered as next time's default) and it books there with the activity named `<plant id> <plant name> - <activity>`. Every entry also gets a detailed **Notes** field: added units by name, renames as `old → new`, which params were tuned per device, plant-setting changes **with values** (`packet_interval (AK3): 400 → 4000` — anything that looks like a password/secret shows only "changed"), and per-drawing `rev X → Y · layout edited`; a session with no config commit still gets a note like `Worked via phpMyAdmin ×3 · VNC`. Internal machinery (Data Engine / sysinfo tables) never appears as "work". The task matcher understands Norwegian↔English discipline names and your device vocabulary (Danfoss 080Z/084B/EKC/AK-CC ⇒ refrigeration, Exhausto/OJ ⇒ ventilation, CGE/EM2 ⇒ energy — calibrated on 37 real plant-days and the standard project template): completed ✓ tasks lose evidence ties to open ones, on equal evidence the more specific task wins (`Design: Ventilation` beats `Design: Heating/ VGV`), a bare-named task like `Nytt oversiktsbilde` inherits its discipline from the board category it sits in (*Refrigeration and freezing systems*), and sales-order quantity lines (`Per energy meter — 3 pcs`, `IWMAC Aftermarket: …`) are never booking targets. **Creating a new activity is a last resort**: the matcher also knows Norwegian operational task names (`Konfigurering`, `Igangkjøring`, `Oppstart`, `Integrasjon: …`; `Server configured` / `Port forwarding` count as Setup), and when the strict match fails a rescue pass books onto any open task whose name **or board category** fits the day's evidence — and rather than give up on a tie it takes a deterministic **best guess** (marked *(best guess)* in the plan row so you can eyeball it before booking). Checklist rows (approvals, `Alarm test`, sales/handover/documentation) are never picked, and no two categories land on the same task. On finished projects where every task is ✓-completed, the guess books onto the completed task rather than creating an activity, and a day with no config evidence at all guesses by your discipline order (refrigeration first) — so a pure Designer day lands on `Design: Refrigeration`, not a `Drawing: Designer session` activity. Only a project with nothing bookable left still gets a new activity. Quick checks are never booked, a project+category already on that date is skipped (so re-clicking can't double-book), archived duplicate projects are ignored, and a **RAC** plant/project books its integration remainder under **Setup - PC / Gateway** instead. Each individual plant row also shows its **own** category split as small colour-dot chips (e.g. `Integration 53m · Drawing 24m · Setup 18m`), so you can see which plant fed each category. The mapping reads the *kind* of work, not just the clicks: **Designer** and **AK3-scanner setup** log very few pang clicks but take real time, so each is credited a nominal chunk (AK3 ≈ 18 min/use; **Designer = the designer session** — from opening the designer until you leave the plant, bridging a quick pop-out to phpMyAdmin/VNC and back but stopping at a real pause; and if the plant's own config **commit** lands within 20 min of the session's end, the session extends to that save (a quick glance at another plant no longer swallows the drawing time) — or 8 min, whichever is larger) carved off the visit's minutes; the remainder goes to **Integration** when there's real config evidence (a non-graphic config commit, phpMyAdmin, or System tools), to **Drawing** if the visit was graphic-only, to **Setup** if it was AK3-only, otherwise a short access-only visit (under ~15 min — you most likely just popped in to check something) is a **Quick check**: shown on the row and in the roll-up for awareness, but **left out of the booked total**. Crucially, adding a device commits the graphic table *and* the device tables together (measured: 457/457 config commits across 94 plants are device/integration changes), so a commit can never tell graphic work apart from device work — **Drawing time therefore comes from the Designer *actions* — specifically the time you spend in the designer between opening it and your next pang click**, not the commits. This is **plant work only**: meetings, admin/planning, documentation and training never touch pang, so they're left out — add those lines yourself. Treat the hours as an estimate and adjust before submitting.

**⤴ Book week** (v4.93): a button injected **left of Rocketlane's own "Add"** on the My Timesheet toolbar books the whole week in one go. It builds the same booking plan for **Monday–Friday** (cached full-scan data when available, else a quick scan; future days are skipped), distributes each day to your **Workday total (default 7,5 h)** across that day's bookable plants, and shows one review list — day headers with `N to book · 7h 30m · ⏭ M already booked`, a tickbox per row, `‹ ›` to switch weeks — then books everything ticked. **It never duplicates**: anything already on the sheet for that project & category & date shows as ⏭ and is skipped, and if the what's-already-booked check fails for a day, that whole day is locked out rather than risk a double entry. Plants without a project of their own get the same team-bucket picker as Book day, right in the week view: choose a **Team … Oppgaver** project (your last choice is preselected), tick the row, and it books there as `<plant id> <plant name> - <activity>`.

The time split is estimated from your clicks (pang logs clicks, not active time), with one refinement from the change log: a **sub-tool config session** — phpMyAdmin / Designer / Direct / VNC work that logs almost no pang clicks but commits a real config change — would otherwise show as ~1 min. When a sparse-click visit that opened one of those tools has a real config commit, it's credited the actual session span (first click → the commit, 5–20 min) instead — and if a long idle had already inflated the click estimate to the 30-min cap, the commit now pulls it back **down** to that span. Click-heavy plants are unchanged. Conversely, a **single** click that just opened a plant or a tool (Direct / VNC / phpMyAdmin / System tools) and committed *nothing* is treated as a quick check (capped at ~8 min) rather than inheriting the full 30-min gap credit. And a silence of over ~45 minutes after a click only keeps its full 30-min credit when the plant's change log shows a config commit during it — an unevidenced long silence is usually a break or a meeting, so it counts as ~15 min instead. Optionally set your **Workday total** and tick **Distribute to total** to split the day's hours across your **bookable** plants weighted by that activity (quick checks are shown but kept out of the distributed total, so "to book" matches your workday).

Each plant row also shows a 🔧 **changes** badge when IWMAC recorded config commits *during your active window* on that plant — a fast way to tell plants you actually configured from ones you only opened. **Click the badge** to expand a drawer showing *what* changed, read live from pang's config-commit history. The drawer groups changes into collapsible sections, **all collapsed by default** so you can drill into just what you care about — in order: **Plant units** (named by the unit, e.g. `Belimo Energimåler (1)`), **Graphic** (e.g. `Graphic Hovedoversikt: rev 4 → 5 · layout edited`), **Plant settings** (e.g. `packet_interval (AK3): 400 → 4000`), and **More changes** (driver parameters, devices, virtual values…). Click a section header to expand it; long sections reveal ~10 changes at a time behind a clickable **“+N more changes”** link, so you can keep clicking to dig deeper without being flooded. Lines are colour-coded: green = added, red = removed, blue = changed. When a lot of units are added or removed at once the line collapses to a count (`+ 38 units added`) with a **“show all”** link to list every one, and a bulk identical change in *More changes* (e.g. a regroup that bumps the alarm group on dozens of rows) collapses to a single `group: 3 → 6 (44 rows)` line instead of dozens of near-identical ones. The drawer also **keeps its place**: if you expand sections or reveal more lines and then change the workday total (which redraws the list), it reopens exactly where you left off. Commits are automatic `:system:` snapshots with no author recorded, so this shows what the config changed to *around* your visit — not necessarily changes you personally made. The badge counts only **change-triggered** commits: scheduled snapshots (the hourly `:00`/`:01` ones, plus nightly and daily) are filtered out as noise, so a long visit no longer over-counts just because an hourly snapshot happened to land inside it.

## How it works

The panel runs on Rocketlane and calls pang's `actions.php` (`method:"get_history"`) once per plant in scope via `GM_xmlhttpRequest` (batched, http origin — see below). **Search** scopes to your recent plants plus your accumulated footprint (`user_plants`: every plant you've been matched on); **Full scan** scopes to the whole inventory and feeds that footprint. Since pang has no server-side date filter, there's no way to shrink the per-plant response — keeping the everyday scope small (footprint) is what keeps it fast. For the date on screen it additionally calls the changes backend (`commits.php`, `method:"get_commits"`) for just the shown plants and tags each with any config commits that land inside your active window (the 🔧 badge); those commits are automatic `:system:` snapshots, so the badge is a time-correlation, not an authored-by signal. Each commit is classified by its timestamp — scheduled snapshots (hourly `:00`/`:01`, nightly, daily) vs off-the-hour change-triggered ones — and only the change-triggered ones count toward the badge; a change-triggered commit on a sparse sub-tool session also feeds the time estimate (see above). Clicking the badge drills in via two more changes services — `tables.php` (`get_tables_patch`: which tables a commit touched, with a `mode` flag) and `data.php` (`get_two_versions`: old vs new content) — classifying noise/blobs/added-devices by table name *before* fetching, so only real parameter tables are downloaded and diffed (by column name, so structural changes don't misalign).

The full inventory (plant ids + names) only exists in a live pang tab, websocket-loaded into `module_plants.coll.data` — there's no HTTP endpoint that lists plants. So the first full scan briefly opens `pang.qxs` in the foreground to harvest that list, then caches it for reuse.

## Limitations

- Pang's API is per-plant (`get_history(plant_id)`) and has no server-side "what did user X do on date Y" filter, so every plant's full history is read and filtered client-side. To keep this fast the script **batches** the reads (JSON-RPC, ~10 plants per request, 20 workers — measured 1.6× faster than bigger batches, since pang serializes each batch and large ones block the browser's ~6 connections; a Full scan lands around ~30 s). The changes log is fetched the same way but as **parallel single requests** (3.2× faster than a batch when the server's cache is cold) and each plant's commit list is **cached for the session** — one list covers every date, so browsing a week only fetches it once. Full-scan results are cached per date (`full_scan_cache`), so re-opening a previously full-scanned day is instant. Today's cache can go stale as you keep working — the footer shows the scan time, and Full scan always re-runs and overwrites it.
- The plant list itself only exists in a live pang tab (websocket-loaded into `module_plants.coll.data`; no HTTP endpoint, and IDs are sparse — 203 up past 50000 — so a numeric range scan isn't viable). The first full scan opens pang in the foreground for ~6 s to harvest the inventory, then caches it (`all_plants`), so the flash rarely repeats.
- **Refresh** (and the default auto-load) is fast but only covers your ~50 recent pang plants plus your footprint — it misses any plant you didn't open through pang (e.g. plant-admin/designer visits). Use **Full scan** to catch those.
- pang is served on both **http and https**, with *separate* per-origin browser storage — so the recent list and login differ between the two (the full inventory + history are identical). The script copies recent + login into shared Tampermonkey storage and, on first use, harvests from **both** protocols, so it works whichever one a colleague uses.
- **Identity matching.** History entries are tagged with a `user` field that pang derives from the auth cookie (`iw_security[username]`) — bare for some logins (`thomas.kvalvag`), email for others (`eivind.slordal@kiona.com`). The script reads that same cookie (not the SPA's `pang.login.username`, which can be missing or formatted differently) and normalises both sides (lowercase, strip `@domain`) before matching. If a date has no activity for you, the panel simply shows a **"No data for &lt;date&gt;"** message (a Quick scan also nudges you to try a Full scan, since your visit may be on a plant not in your recent list).
